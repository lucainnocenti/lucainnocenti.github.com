Method · interpretation · model assumptions
Each realization draws a Haar-isometric frame Q ∈ ℂn_out×d (Q†Q = I), giving rank-one effects Ea = Q†|a⟩⟨a|Q. It is the same POVM for every training state and σ in that realization. Training states are Haar pure; the rank-one target O and test state σ are fixed across the map by default, with a switch for exact Haar integration of both. True training labels are yi = Tr(ρiO). If enabled, the observed labels for regression are yi + εi, with independent εi ∼ N(0, γ²), even for N = ∞; the MSE is measured against the noiseless target Tr(σO). The fit is the minimum-norm Moore–Penrose solution w = (PT)+yobserved, computed by a direct one-sided Jacobi SVD (not normal equations). No ridge regularization is applied; rcond only truncates numerically zero singular values.
The default plotted sample for each training realization is the test-shot-averaged MSE: (w·pσ − yσ)² + [Σₐ pσ,ₐ wₐ² − (w·pσ)²]/M. Thus the finite M contribution is included exactly, without extra Monte Carlo scatter. “Sample test shots” instead draws one measured frequency vector per realization, except in Haar-average mode where exact integration is enforced. The Haar-average mode uses the pure-state second moment E[O⊗O]=(I+SWAP)/[d(d+1)] and performs the exact O, σ and independent Gaussian label-noise averages conditional on each training dataset, POVM and estimated P; finite-M test-shot variance is also included analytically. For depolarization λ the test state is (1−λ)|ψ⟩⟨ψ|+λI/d, with |ψ⟩ Haar pure. Only the training states, POVM and N-shot training noise are Monte Carlo sampled in this mode. Mean/median refer to the distribution of these conditional Haar-averaged MSEs, not the distribution of individual random test errors. Training uses either genuine multinomial frequencies or Gaussian noise with covariance (diag p − ppᵀ)/N, including its sum-to-one constraint. All draws use a seeded pseudorandom generator. Mean and median aggregate per-realization squared errors, not squared mean predictions. The separate squared-bias and inference-variance plots show the ensemble mean of each component; component medians are not stored in the precomputed datasets. The rank-one-POVM domain requires n_out ≥ d. The outcome and training ranges are independently editable in integer counts or multiples of d². Sample density can be any positive safe integer, or every physically admissible integer in the chosen domain; parameter inputs are not subject to arbitrary upper caps. The separate axis controls optionally fix the displayed n_out, n_tr and raw value ranges independently of the computed domain. A fixed plotted-value range also fixes the heatmap/3D color limits; log plots transform those raw bounds. The physical restrictions d ≥ 2, n_out ≥ d, n_tr ≥ 1, N and M ≥ 1, γ ≥ 0, and 0 ≤ λ ≤ 1 remain. Counts and random seeds are limited to exactly represented JavaScript integers, and the pseudorandom generator uses the seed modulo 2³². The background grid uses distinct integer counts, spaced as uniformly as integer counts allow; each axis is capped by its own number of admissible counts. When the sample count permits, the d² coordinate is retained on each axis if it lies in the selected range. Horizontal slices fix n_tr and vary n_out; vertical slices fix n_out and vary n_tr. Slice mode computes only the selected rows or columns, and multiple fixed values can be compared on one plot. When enabled, critical scans separately evaluate every integer on n_out = n_tr, n_out = d², and n_tr = d² within the domain, plus one-count neighbors; slices sample the corresponding intersections and their neighbors. Circles on the joined surface/heatmap mark scans, including off-grid samples. The 3D joined surface linearly connects adjacent background grid values. The 3D discrete bars mode draws every computed integer pair, including critical scans, without interpolating bar heights. Bar footprints meet at midpoint boundaries between neighboring outcome columns and, within each column, neighboring training counts. Sparse sampling therefore produces wider bars, while each height remains the statistic evaluated at its sampled integer pair. Color stretch may be robust (2nd–98th percentiles) or full range; neither alters reported values. Log colors floor positive values at 10−20.
Near n_tr ≈ n_out, single-experiment errors can be heavy-tailed, making the sample mean sensitive to the realization count. The IC line n_out = d² and the training-span line n_tr = d² delimit exact-reconstruction capacity, but do not guarantee a small error at finite shots. At large d, fine grids and many realizations require significant CPU work. The test-depolarization slider changes only σ, not the pure training ensemble. The Haar-average mode treats Gaussian training-label noise analytically, even when γ ≠ 0; the original fixed/redrawn modes continue sampling label noise.
