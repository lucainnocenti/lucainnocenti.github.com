About this visualization: current setup, how to read the plots, model and estimator

Training states on the Z-axis or random on the Bloch sphere, a random rank-1 POVM, finite shots N and M (either may be infinite), and label noise γ shape the distribution of ridge predictions for an unseen test state.

Current setup

Random rank-1 POVM. The full A-outcome features enter regression; only the first three probabilities appear in the 3D view.
Z-axis: K equally spaced Bloch vectors from +Z to −Z, including mixed states. Switch to the sphere for uniformly random pure states.
Exact independent multinomial draws for 5 training states and the test state. Label noise stays Gaussian. With N = ∞ or M = ∞, the corresponding frequencies are exact.

How to interpret the geometry

Projected covariance contours reflect the first three measured probabilities. Their characteristic widths scale as \(N^{-1/2}\) (test: \(M^{-1/2}\)). When \(A>3\) the first three coordinates need not sum to one: the permitted projected region is a tetrahedron, not a triangle.

Singular-value curves compare full \(A\)-dimensional noisy and exact feature matrices, with/without centering across the \(K\) training states. Centering removes the constant-probability direction.

Spectral conditioning. Z-axis training generates at most two exact feature directions. Generic sphere training and \(A\ge 4\) can support four affine directions.

Regularization risk. The MSE curve recomputes the prediction for every listed λ using the same training/test sampling and noisy labels, and compares each prediction with the exact test expectation. \(\operatorname{MSE}=\operatorname{Bias}^2+\operatorname{Var}\).

Informational completeness. Two or three outcomes cannot determine all Bloch components. Generic rank-1 POVMs with \(A\ge 4\) outcomes can be informationally complete, but training restricted to the Z-axis still cannot identify responses to arbitrary off-axis states.

Model and estimator
States and rank-1 POVM
\(\rho_k=\frac12\bigl(I+\mathbf r_k\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\bigr)\)
Z-axis: \(\mathbf r_k=\left(0,0,1-\frac{2(k-1)}{K-1}\right)\)
Sphere: \(\|\mathbf r_k\|=1,\quad z\sim\mathcal U[-1,1],\quad\phi\sim\mathcal U[0,2\pi)\)
\(\mu_a=\frac12\bigl(t_a I+\mathbf v_a\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\bigr)\)
\(\mathbf v_a=t_a\mathbf n_a,\qquad\|\mathbf n_a\|=1\)
Two measurement-sampling models
\(p_{ka}=\operatorname{Tr}(\mu_a\rho_k)\)
Multinomial: \(\mathbf C_k\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(N,\mathbf p_k)\)
\(\hat{\mathbf p}_k=\mathbf C_k/N\)
Gaussian: \(\hat{\mathbf p}_k\sim\mathcal N\!\left(\mathbf p_k,\,\frac{\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf p_k)-\mathbf p_k\mathbf p_k^{\mathsf T}}{N}\right)\)
For the test state, replace \(N\) with \(M\).
Noisy training labels and ridge fit
\(\hat y_k=\operatorname{Tr}(O\rho_k)+\gamma\xi_k\)
\(\xi_k\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,1)\)
\(\hat{\mathbf w}_\lambda=\bigl(\hat P^{\mathsf T}\hat P+\lambda I\bigr)^{+}\hat P^{\mathsf T}\hat{\mathbf y}\)
\(\hat{\mathbf w}_\lambda\in\mathbb R^A\); \({}^{+}\) denotes Moore–Penrose inverse.
Test prediction and exact target
\(p_a(\sigma)=\operatorname{Tr}(\mu_a\sigma)\)
\(\hat o_\lambda=\hat{\mathbf p}(\sigma)^{\mathsf T}\hat{\mathbf w}_\lambda\)
\(o_{\mathrm{true}}=\operatorname{Tr}(O\sigma)\)
\(N=\infty:\ \hat{\mathbf p}_k=\mathbf p_k;\quad M=\infty:\ \hat{\mathbf p}(\sigma)=\mathbf p(\sigma)\)
\(\operatorname{MSE}(\lambda)=\mathbb E[(\hat o_\lambda-o_{\mathrm{true}})^2]\)

All \(A\) outcomes enter sampling, fitting and prediction; the 3D plot shows only \((p_1,p_2,p_3)\), without renormalization. When \(A=2\), the third coordinate is zero. Every Monte Carlo run has independent training and test measurements and \(K\) independent training-label perturbations of variance \(\gamma^2\). Test labels are never perturbed.

The Gaussian sampler matches the multinomial mean and covariance and preserves \(\sum_a\hat p_a=1\), but does not enforce \(\hat p_a\ge0\) at small shot counts. The conditional test-shot contribution is \(\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(\hat o\mid\hat{\mathbf w})=\frac{1}{M}\left[\sum_a p_a(\sigma)\hat w_a^2-\bigl(\mathbf p(\sigma)^{\mathsf T}\hat{\mathbf w}\bigr)^2\right].\) With \(M=\infty\), test features are exact. Locked prediction axes retain the same limits as parameters vary; select Refit lock to rescale.
